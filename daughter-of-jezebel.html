The Story

A Kingdom Bound by Marriage—and Divided by Allegiance

When Jehoshaphat binds Judah to the powerful house of Ahab, the marriage is meant to secure peace. Instead, Athaliah brings the political instincts of Omri and the gods of the coast into the house of David.

Across a generation of ambition, murder, rebellion, disease, and war, she proves herself more capable than the men around her. But while she governs the visible kingdom, an old priest and a king’s daughter guard a promise she believes she has destroyed.

From the Opening

The Mountain Fortress

Jerusalem rose out of the pale Judean hills like a fist of limestone thrust against the sky.

Athaliah stood within the curtained litter as the northern procession climbed the last steep road toward the city. Through the slit in the dyed linen, she watched terraced vineyards surrender to walls, towers, and the white blaze of the sanctuary above them.

It did not look like an empire. It looked like a tomb that had refused to stay buried.

The road narrowed beneath the gate. Judean elders waited in ordered ranks, their wool mantles dun against the stone. Beyond them stood priests in white linen, watching the cedar chests of her dowry as though northern silver carried contagion.

Athaliah met their eyes through the veil and did not blink. Let the priests glare. Her mother, Jezebel, had broken far louder men on the slopes of Carmel.
