Scripture and Historical Fiction

History Behind the Novel

Daughter of Jezebel treats the biblical record as its fixed public history, then uses disciplined imagination to enter the rooms, councils, fears, and ambitions the record does not describe.

The Fixed Record

What Comes from Scripture

The alliance between Jehoshaphat and Ahab’s house; Jehoram’s marriage, accession, fratricide, rebellions, illness, and unmourned death; Ahaziah’s northern counsel and alliance at Ramoth-gilead; Jehu’s revolt; Athaliah’s seizure of the throne; Jehoshabeath’s rescue of Joash; six hidden years; and Jehoiada’s covenant and coronation all come from Kings and Chronicles.

The novel also preserves the theological interpretation those books give the events. Idolatry is not merely cultural exchange. Murder brings bloodguilt. Royal power does not cancel covenant, and the survival of David’s house is not an accident of palace intrigue.

The Novelist’s Work

What Has Been Imagined

Household Life

Private conversations, marriages beyond those named, servants, rivalries, ceremonies, and most domestic detail are fictional constructions shaped to fit the public record.

Political Motive

Scripture records decisions and judgments more often than inward reasoning. The novel imagines Athaliah’s Omride political philosophy, Jehoshaphat’s fears, and Jehoram’s dependence without changing the recorded outcome.

Material World

Palace rooms, gates, garments, chariot practice, food, weapons, and routes are historically informed reconstructions. Where a site is disputed, the website and map say so.

Reader’s Guide

Important Historical-Fiction Choices

ChoiceTreatment in the novelReason
Athaliah’s motherThe novel presents Athaliah as Jezebel’s biological daughter.This traditional identification supports the title and the book’s maternal psychology. Scripture clearly places Athaliah in Ahab and Omri’s house but does not explicitly name Jezebel as her mother.
Jehoshabeath’s motherShe is Jehoram’s daughter by another Judean wife.Scripture names her father, brother, and husband but not her mother. This reconstruction preserves those relationships without making Athaliah her mother.
Ahaziah’s ageTwenty-two at accession; twenty-three in his final campaign year.This follows 2 Kings 8:26–27 rather than the variant figure in 2 Chronicles 22:2.
Jehoshabeath’s marriageJehoshaphat arranges it to strengthen Jehoram’s tie to the priesthood.This is an invented motive consistent with the king’s biblical concern for covenant instruction and his fear of northern influence.
Ahinoam and Malki-ramBoth are fictional.They embody Judean and Benjamite resistance inside the royal household and give the succession crisis a human face.
Temple concealmentJehoiada avoids open conflict while Joash is hidden.The silence explains how a stable reign and a concealed heir can coexist: Athaliah considers the priest contained and politically irrelevant; Jehoiada refuses to draw attention to the Temple.

The governing principle: invention is permitted where the biblical record is silent; it is not used to erase the record’s theology, reverse its moral judgments, or secretly replace its stated events.
