The Southern Levant · Ninth Century BC

Map and Chronology

From the mountain fortress of Jerusalem to the chariot roads of Jezreel, the story unfolds across two allied kingdoms moving toward a shared judgment.

The World of the Novel

Judah, Israel, Phoenicia, Gilead, and Edom

Royal cities are marked in red. Gur and Zair are approximate because their precise locations remain uncertain.

Reading the route: The eastern campaign centers on Ramoth-gilead. The western sequence follows the biblical geography of Jezreel, Ibleam, the ascent of Gur, and Megiddo. The map is a reader’s guide, not a claim that every disputed site can be fixed with certainty.
Synchronized Story Chronology

A Generation from Marriage to Restoration

The novel’s internal ages are anchored in Jehoram’s eight-year reign, Ahaziah’s accession at twenty-two in 2 Kings 8:26, and Joash’s coronation at seven.

  1. Year 0
    Athaliah arrives in JerusalemJehoram is about seventeen. The northern alliance enters the household of David.
  2. Year 1
    Ahaziah is bornJehoram is eighteen; Jehoshabeath and Malki-ram are about three.
  3. Year 11
    The princes come of age togetherAhaziah is ten. Rival claims and rival visions of kingship begin to harden.
  4. Year 14
    Jehoshabeath marries JehoiadaJehoshaphat seeks to bind his son’s household to the priesthood before his death.
  5. Year 15
    Jehoram becomes king at thirty-twoAhaziah is fourteen. Jehoram’s eight-year reign begins.
  6. Years 16–21
    Judah’s borders breakElijah’s warning is followed by revolt, invasion, and the ruin of the royal household.
  7. Year 23
    Ahaziah becomes king at twenty-twoHis one-year reign turns toward Ramoth-gilead and the northern court.
  8. Year 24
    Jehu’s revolt reaches both housesAhaziah is twenty-three. The judgment on Ahab’s dynasty crosses into Judah.
  9. Years 24–30
    Athaliah reigns; Joash remains hiddenThe queen’s government is outwardly stable while the heir grows within the Temple.
  10. Year 30
    The seventh yearJoash is crowned at seven, and the public line of David is restored.

About the dates: Ancient accession-year systems and inclusive counting do not behave exactly like modern birthday arithmetic. The sequence preserves the biblical regnal anchors while keeping the characters’ ages internally consistent.
