“And So All Israel Shall Be Saved”

A present remnant, a future ingathering — Paul promises a large-scale turning of Israel to Christ, through the same mercy that saves the Gentiles

By James Burke • July 21, 2026 • 10 min read • Romans 11:25–36
Home › What Does It Mean to Be Saved? › Romans 11:25–36

Romans 9 spoke of a remnant of Israel saved now. Romans 11 lifts its eyes to the future, and the saving word takes on a horizon: not only a remnant preserved through unbelief, but a coming turning of Israel on a scale the present remnant only anticipates.

The Mystery Paul Reveals

For I would not, brethren, that ye should be ignorant of this mystery, lest ye should be wise in your own conceits; that blindness in part is happened to Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in. And so all Israel shall be saved.

Romans 11:25–26

Pas Israēl sōthēsetai — all Israel shall be saved. Paul calls it a mystery — something previously hidden, now disclosed — and he sets it against Gentile pride, lest ye should be wise in your own conceits. The Gentile church is not to gloat over Israel’s present hardening, because that hardening is neither total nor final.

Two limits are built into the sentence. The blindness is in part — not the whole nation, and not for ever. And it lasts until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in — it has a terminus. When the appointed number of Gentiles has been gathered, the partial hardening lifts, and so all Israel shall be saved.

What “All Israel” Means — and Does Not

This is the phrase that must be handled with care, because it is pulled in two wrong directions.

It does not mean that every ethnic Israelite throughout history is automatically saved. That would contradict everything Paul has just spent three chapters establishing — that descent does not secure salvation, that they are not all Israel which are of Israel, that only the remnant of faith is saved. Paul does not reverse himself here and grant blanket salvation by bloodline. The same faith is required.

And it does not mean the church. All Israel is not a coded way of saying all believers, dissolving the ethnic people into a spiritual category. Paul has been talking about ethnic Israel throughout this chapter — his kinsmen according to the flesh, the natural branches, the people presently hardened. To make all Israel suddenly mean the church empties the passage of the very hope Paul is announcing: that God has not cast away His people.

What it means is a future, large-scale turning of the nation of Israel to Christ. Not every individual without exception, but Israel as a people, on a scale that answers to the present remnant as the harvest answers to the firstfruits. The partial hardening gives way, and a great turning comes. That is the mystery: the story of Israel is not closed at the remnant; a national reception is still ahead.

Through the Same Mercy

The crucial point — the one that keeps this from becoming a separate arrangement for Israel — is how they are saved. Paul is explicit:

There shall come out of Sion the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob: for this is my covenant unto them, when I shall take away their sins.

Romans 11:26–27

The Deliverer is Christ. He removes ungodliness; He takes away sins. Israel’s future salvation comes through the same Christ, the same removal of sin, the same mercy by which the Gentiles were brought in. There is no second track. Paul makes the symmetry explicit a few verses on: “For as ye in times past have not believed God, yet have now obtained mercy through their unbelief: even so have these also now not believed, that through your mercy they also may obtain mercy.” (Romans 11:30–31)

Mercy is the single currency. The Gentiles obtained it; Israel will obtain the same mercy. Not a covenant that saves Jews apart from Christ, but the one covenant mercy in Christ, reaching a people presently hardened. This is the whole gateway’s insistence again: one Saviour, one gospel, one mercy — extended, in Israel’s case, to a future turning.

“Concerning the Election, Beloved”

Paul grounds the certainty of this in God’s own fidelity, not Israel’s worthiness:

As concerning the gospel, they are enemies for your sakes: but as touching the election, they are beloved for the fathers’ sakes. For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance.

Romans 11:28–29

Without repentance — God does not take back His gifts or change His mind about His call. Israel is presently, as regards the gospel, in the posture of an enemy; and yet, as regards God’s electing love, beloved for the fathers’ sakes. The future turning is secured not by anything in Israel but by the irrevocable character of God’s calling. What He promised the fathers He will not abandon.

That is why Paul can speak of Israel’s restoration as life from the dead — a resurrection-scale mercy, the reversal of a hardening that looked final. The God who grafts branches back into their own olive tree is the God whose gifts and calling do not lapse.

The Doxology

Paul cannot hold the argument any longer, and it breaks into worship:

O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! … For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen.

Romans 11:33, 36

This is not a retreat from the argument but its proper end. Having traced how God hardens and shows mercy, gathers Gentiles and will gather Israel, Paul does not pretend to have mapped it exhaustively. He worships. The mercy that reaches both peoples is deeper than the mind that receives it, and the only fitting response to it is praise.

A study should end where Paul ends. The mechanics of Israel’s future are not fully given to us; the certainty of God’s mercy is. And that mercy runs, from first to last, through the Deliverer who comes out of Zion.

What the Word Means Here

The full sense — salvation from unbelief and ungodliness into covenant mercy — with a future, corporate horizon. The peril is partial hardening; the result is the removal of ungodliness and restoration; the agent is God through the Deliverer; and the scope is all Israel, understood as a coming large-scale turning of the nation.

Its contribution to this gateway is the horizon it adds and the guardrails it keeps. Salvation has a future for Israel that is real and secured by God’s irrevocable calling — not automatic by descent, not absorbed into the church, and not by any track other than faith in the Christ who takes away sin. The same mercy that saved the Gentiles will turn away ungodliness from Jacob. One Deliverer, one mercy, and a people not yet cast away.
