“Thou Shalt Save Both Thyself, and Them That Hear Thee”

A minister’s persevering attention to his life and doctrine is made an instrument of salvation — his own, and his hearers’

By James Burke • July 21, 2026 • 8 min read • 1 Timothy 4:6–16
Home › What Does It Mean to Be Saved? › 1 Timothy 4:6–16

Paul is instructing a young minister on how to conduct himself in the church, and he ends the charge with a sentence that puts salvation — Timothy’s own and his hearers’ — in the outcome of that conduct. It is one of the New Testament’s clearest statements of pastoral ministry as an instrument of salvation.

The Charge

Paul has warned Timothy of coming apostasy — some departing from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits. Against that, he tells Timothy to be a good minister of Jesus Christ, nourished up in the words of faith and of good doctrine, to exercise himself unto godliness, to be an example in word, conduct, love, faith, purity, and to give attendance to reading, exhortation, and doctrine. Then the summary charge:

Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine; continue in them: for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself, and them that hear thee.

1 Timothy 4:16

Sōseis kai seauton kai tous akouontas sou — thou shalt save both thyself, and them that hear thee. Two objects of the saving, and one means: taking heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine, and continuing in them. The salvation of the minister and of his hearers is bound up with his persevering attention to two things — his own life, and his teaching.

The Two Things Timothy Must Watch

Paul names them deliberately, and both are needed.

Thyself — the minister’s own life, character, and godliness. A man cannot be an instrument of others’ salvation while neglecting his own soul; his life is part of his message. The doctrine — the teaching, the content preached. Sound life without sound doctrine, or sound doctrine without a life to match, each fails. Timothy must watch both, and continue in them — the perseverance word again, the same steady holding-on the gateway keeps meeting.

The context makes the urgency plain. False teaching is spreading; some are departing from the faith. In that setting, a minister’s careful attention to his life and his doctrine is not routine maintenance — it is the means by which he and his hearers are kept in saving truth rather than swept into destructive error.

“Save Thyself” — in What Sense

The phrase save thyself needs care, because it can be misheard as though a minister earns his own salvation by diligence. That is not Paul’s meaning, and the whole of his theology forbids it. Paul, who insists salvation is not by works of righteousness which we have done, is not here making Timothy his own saviour.

What he means is preservation in the faith, along the road of ministry. Timothy is saved by grace through faith, as anyone is; and Paul is saying that a minister who attends carefully to his life and doctrine will be kept from the shipwreck of faith that his careless contemporaries are suffering. To save himself is to persevere — to not be among those who depart from the faith — by the God-appointed means of watching his life and his teaching. The salvation is God’s; the perseverance is the road; and neglecting the road is how ministers make shipwreck.

This matches the note Paul strikes about his own ministry, where he disciplines his body lest … I myself should be a castaway. The one who preaches salvation to others is not exempt from the call to persevere, and his perseverance runs through exactly the diligence Paul here prescribes.

And “Them That Hear Thee”

The other object is the hearers. A faithful minister, taking heed to his life and doctrine, is a genuine instrument in the salvation of those who listen to him. God saves; but He saves through the preached word, and the one who preaches it faithfully is the means by which his hearers are kept in and brought to saving truth.

This is the instrumental theme at its most concentrated. Paul does not say Timothy’s ministry might be helpful; he says Timothy will save his hearers by it. The word is genuine. A minister who guards his life and his doctrine is, under God, saving the people who hear him — and a minister who neglects them endangers not only himself but them.

What the Word Means Here

The full sense — preservation in saving truth against apostasy and destructive error — with the accent, as in the other instrumentality passages, on human agency. The peril is false teaching and departure from the faith; the means is persevering attention to one’s life and doctrine; the agent is God, working through faithful pastoral labour.

Its contribution to the gateway is the double object of the saving: both thyself, and them that hear thee. A minister’s perseverance is not a private matter. Watching his life and his doctrine, and continuing in them, is the means by which he is himself kept in the faith and his hearers are kept and brought to salvation. Not by his own power — the salvation is God’s — but as the real instrument God uses, which is why Paul makes the whole of the charge hang on it.
