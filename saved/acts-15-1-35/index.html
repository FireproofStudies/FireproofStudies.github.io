“Ye Cannot Be Saved” — the Claim the Council Rejected

A teaching that circumcision was necessary to be saved forced the church to say, once and for all, how salvation comes

By James Burke • July 21, 2026 • 9 min read • Acts 15:1–35
Home › What Does It Mean to Be Saved? › Acts 15:1–35

This passage does not narrate a salvation. It records a claim about how salvation comes, judges it false, and in doing so gives the clearest statement in Acts of the ground on which anyone is saved at all.

The Claim

And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved.

Acts 15:1

Ou dynasthe sōthēnai — ye cannot be saved. The saving verb appears here inside an error, on the lips of men adding a condition to the gospel. Their claim was not that circumcision helped, or honoured tradition, or eased fellowship. It was that without it salvation was impossible. They had made a ceremony a requirement for being saved.

This is the sharpest form the works question can take, and Luke lets it stand in its full severity before answering it, because the answer defines the gospel by contrast.

Why It Was So Dangerous

The teaching was plausible. These were not pagans; they appealed to Moses, to Scripture, to the covenant God Himself had given Abraham with circumcision as its sign. The demand had the weight of the Old Testament apparently behind it.

And it was catastrophic, because it changed what salvation rested on. If a Gentile must be circumcised and keep the law to be saved, then salvation is no longer received by grace through faith in Christ; it is completed by a human work, and Christ’s death is made insufficient. Paul saw the whole gospel at stake in it, and had no small dissension and disputation with them.

Peter’s Testimony

The Council convenes, and Peter stands on what he had already watched God do at Cornelius’s house.

But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.

Acts 15:11

Note the direction of the sentence. Peter does not say the Gentiles will be saved the way the Jews are; he says we — the Jewish believers — shall be saved the same way they are: through the grace of the Lord Jesus. He puts himself on the Gentiles’ footing, not the reverse. The ground is identical, and it is grace, and it is the only ground either has.

He drives it home by calling the added requirement a yoke which neither they nor their fathers were able to bear, and asking why they would now lay it on the disciples’ necks — as though tempting God by testing whether He had really saved the Gentiles He had so plainly saved.

James and the Verdict

James, presiding, seals it. He cites the prophets — God visiting the Gentiles to take out a people for His name — and gives his sentence: “that we trouble not them, which from among the Gentiles are turned to God.” The letter that follows lays on them no burden of circumcision or law-keeping, only a few practical restraints for the sake of fellowship.

The claim of verse 1 is thus formally rejected by the whole church. Salvation is not conditioned on circumcision or law observance. It comes through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, received by faith, the same for every kind of person.

What the Word Means Here

The full sense of salvation — but defined, unusually, by what it excludes. The occurrence is a false claim, and the passage exists to correct it. To be saved is not to be circumcised, not to take up the law of Moses, not to add any human work to Christ. It is to receive, by faith, the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ.

Its place in this gateway is essential precisely because it is negative. Many passages say what salvation is; this one guards the door against the most respectable counterfeit — the addition of a religious requirement to grace — and it does so with the united authority of the apostolic church. Whenever cannot be saved is attached to a human condition Christ did not attach, Acts 15 has already ruled on it.

At a Glance

Saved from what?The false yoke of law-keeping as a condition of acceptance
Saved into what?Salvation by grace through faith, the same for Jew and Gentile
Who saves?The Lord Jesus, through whose grace all are saved alike
What was the false claim?That circumcision and law-keeping were necessary to be saved
Comprehensive salvation in Christ?Yes — defined here by exclusion, against a works-added gospel
