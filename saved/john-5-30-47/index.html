“These Things I Say, That Ye Might Be Saved”

Jesus lays out the witnesses to His identity — and names His purpose in doing so as their salvation, not His own vindication

By James Burke • July 21, 2026 • 8 min read • John 5:30–47
Home › What Does It Mean to Be Saved? › John 5:30–47

In the middle of a long defence of His authority, Jesus pauses to say why He is making it. The reason is the saving word.

The Purpose Stated

Jesus has just claimed the right to give life and to judge, and He anticipates the objection that a man cannot vouch for himself. So He assembles witnesses. But first He states His motive:

But I receive not testimony from man: but these things I say, that ye might be saved.

John 5:34

Hina hymeis sōthēte — that ye might be saved. He does not marshal the evidence to win an argument or protect His reputation — I receive not testimony from man. He does it for the sake of the people resisting Him. The whole apologetic that follows is, in His own words, an act of mercy aimed at their salvation.

That reframes everything after it. What looks like a courtroom defence is a rescue attempt.

Four Witnesses

Jesus then names the testimony converging on His identity, and a study of this passage does well simply to follow it, because the point is cumulative.

John the Baptist. “He was a burning and a shining light: and ye were willing for a season to rejoice in his light.” A witness they had once welcomed, whose testimony to Jesus they had since set aside.

The works. “The works which the Father hath given me to finish, the same works that I do, bear witness of me, that the Father hath sent me.” What He does testifies to who sent Him.

The Father. “And the Father himself, which hath sent me, hath borne witness of me.” The deepest testimony, and the one they had never truly heard.

The Scriptures. “Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me.” The writings they prized above all were pointing, the whole time, at the One standing in front of them.

Four witnesses, and they agree. Jesus is not asking for blind trust; He is pointing at a body of testimony and asking why, with all of it, they will still not come.

The Refusal Named

And that is where the passage locates the failure — not in the evidence, which is ample, but in the will.

And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.

John 5:40

It is one of the saddest sentences in the Gospel, and the diagnosis is precise. The problem is not that life is unavailable, or that the witnesses are unclear, or that Jesus has withheld anything. Ye will not come. The life is in Him, freely, and they decline to approach it.

This is what salvation looks like from its human side in John: coming to Christ. Not achieving, not qualifying — coming. And its refusal is likewise an act of the will, a will not, which no amount of further evidence reaches. Jesus tells them plainly that the Scriptures they searched for life were testifying of the very One they would not come to for it.

Why They Could Not Believe

Jesus adds a probe that goes underneath the will: “How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another, and seek not the honour that cometh from God only?”

The obstacle He names is not intellectual but moral — a craving for one another’s approval that leaves no room to receive God’s verdict on His Son. People who live on human esteem cannot afford to believe something that would cost them it. The refusal to come has roots, and Jesus exposes them.

What the Word Means Here

The full sense: salvation as coming to Christ for life, set against the death of refusing Him. That ye might be saved is Jesus’ stated purpose in gathering every witness to Himself.

Its contribution to the gateway is the shape it gives to faith. Salvation here is not primarily assent to propositions, though the propositions are true and witnessed. It is coming — approaching the person the witnesses point to, for the life that is in Him alone. And its refusal is not for lack of evidence but for want of will, a will bound by the love of human approval. Jesus said all of it, He tells them, so that they might be saved.

At a Glance

Saved from what?Death and the darkness of refusing to come to Christ
Saved into what?Life — the life He came to give
Who saves?Jesus, to whom every witness points
Through what means?Coming to Him, in response to the testimony
Comprehensive salvation in Christ?Yes. Salvation expressed as coming to Christ for life
