Saved in Context

“A Remnant Shall Be Saved”

Out of an unbelieving nation God calls a remnant — saved by faith, not descent, and stumbling is the danger for those who pursue righteousness by works

By James Burke • July 21, 2026 • 9 min read • Romans 9:24–33
Home › What Does It Mean to Be Saved? › Romans 9:24–33

Paul is deep in the hardest stretch of Romans, wrestling with why his own people, to whom the promises belong, have largely not received their Messiah. The saving word appears in a quotation from Isaiah, and it is deliberately modest: not all Israel, but a remnant.

Mercy on Both Jew and Gentile

The paragraph opens by naming whom God is calling: “Even us, whom he hath called, not of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles.”

That is the frame. The vessels of mercy God is preparing for glory are drawn from both peoples — Jews and Gentiles alike, called by the same mercy. Paul supports it from Hosea: a people who were not my people become the children of the living God. The Gentiles, once outside, are brought in. And then, turning to Israel, Paul does not claim the whole nation but a portion of it.

“A Remnant Shall Be Saved”

Esaias also crieth concerning Israel, Though the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, a remnant shall be saved.

Romans 9:27

To hypoleimma sōthēsetai — the remnant shall be saved. The word is carefully limited. Against the vast number of ethnic Israel — as the sand of the sea, the very language of the covenant promise to Abraham — Paul sets a saved remnant. Not the nation as a whole in its present unbelief, but a portion preserved through it.

This is one of the load-bearing distinctions in Paul’s whole treatment of Israel, and he has already stated it plainly a few verses earlier: they are not all Israel, which are of Israel. Physical descent from Abraham does not by itself place a person among the saved. There is an Israel within Israel — a remnant according to the election of grace — and it is this remnant that the saving word describes here.

The point is not that God’s promise has failed. It is that the promise was never a guarantee of automatic salvation to every ethnic descendant. It always ran through God’s call and through faith, and the remnant is the proof that it is still running exactly there.

Why Most of Israel Missed It

Paul then names the reason for the larger failure, and it is the same reason the whole gateway keeps meeting: the difference between faith and works.

What shall we say then? That the Gentiles, which followed not after righteousness, have attained to righteousness, even the righteousness which is of faith. But Israel, which followed after the law of righteousness, hath not attained to the law of righteousness.

Romans 9:30–31

It is a stunning reversal, and Paul states it starkly. The Gentiles, who were not even pursuing righteousness, received it — because they received it by faith. Israel, earnestly pursuing righteousness, did not arrive at it. And Paul gives the precise reason: “Because they sought it not by faith, but as it were by the works of the law.”

The failure was not lack of zeal; Israel had zeal. It was the method. They pursued righteousness as something to be achieved by works, and righteousness is not available on those terms — not to them, not to anyone. The remnant that is saved is saved the same way the Gentiles are: by faith, receiving what cannot be earned.

The Stumbling-Stone

Paul locates the whole failure at a single point — a stone laid in Zion.

For they stumbled at that stumblingstone; as it is written, Behold, I lay in Sion a stumblingstone and rock of offence: and whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.

Romans 9:32–33

The stone is Christ. To those who come by faith He is the sure foundation; to those who come by works He is an obstacle they trip over. The same Christ is a rock of refuge and a rock of offence, and which He is to a person depends entirely on whether that person believes on Him or stumbles at Him.

This is why Israel’s failure is not finally about ancestry or effort but about a person. They stumbled over the Messiah — a crucified Christ was an offence, a scandal to a righteousness sought by works — and in stumbling over Him they missed the righteousness they were chasing. The remnant is precisely those who did not stumble but believed: whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.

What This Does Not Say

A study of this passage must be careful in two directions, because Romans 9 is handled carelessly from both.

It does not say ethnic Israel is finished. Paul is describing the present situation — a nation largely in unbelief, with a saved remnant within it — and he will spend chapter 11 insisting God has not cast away His people. The remnant now is not the end of the story of Israel; it is one chapter of it.

And it does not say salvation runs on two tracks, one for Jews and another for Gentiles. The whole force of the paragraph is that there is one way in: faith in the Christ laid in Zion. The remnant of Israel and the called of the Gentiles are saved by the same mercy, through the same faith, in the same stone. There is no separate arrangement by which Israel is saved apart from believing on Christ.

What the Word Means Here

The full sense — salvation from unbelief and judgment into God’s saving promise — but named as a remnant. The peril is national unbelief and stumbling over Christ; the means is faith rather than works; the agent is God, who calls and preserves a people through the failure of the many.

Its contribution to this gateway is the corrective it puts on any easy assumption about who is saved. Descent does not secure it; zeal does not secure it; only faith in the stone laid in Zion does — and by that faith God has always kept a remnant. That the remnant is small now is not the failure of the promise. It is the promise doing exactly what it always did: saving those who believe, from every people, Jew and Gentile alike.

At a Glance

Saved from what?National unbelief, judgment, and stumbling over Christ
Saved into what?God’s saving promise, entered by faith
Who saves?God, who calls and preserves a remnant
Through what means?Faith in Christ, not the pursuit of righteousness by works
Comprehensive salvation in Christ?Yes — remnant salvation from within unbelieving Israel
